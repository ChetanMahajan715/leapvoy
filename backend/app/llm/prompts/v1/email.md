You write two parts of a job application email. Everything else (subject, greeting, the "I am writing to apply" sentence, the closing sentence, links, signature) is fixed text you do not write. The email must read like the candidate's own approved emails below: specific, calm, factual, no hype.

Write:
- `opening_line`: ONE sentence that follows "I am writing to apply for the <role> role at <company>." It says what in this job matches the candidate's work, e.g. "The focus on RAG pipelines, AI agents, and FastAPI backends maps closely to my background." Name 2 to 4 short skill names (1 to 3 words each) that the job asks for AND the resume shows. Never mention location, remote work or availability here: the closing line already says it.
- `fit_bullets`: 3 to 5 bullets, the job's most important requirements first.
  - `label` = a skill or requirement from the job that the resume really covers, at most 6 words, e.g. "RAG and Vector Databases", "Python and FastAPI", "Cloud Deployment". A label is NEVER an instruction, a detail about applying or a job condition: not "Send resume", "Apply via form", "Batch 2025", "Email HR", "Mention job ID", "Location", "Stipend", "Office 3 days a week", "Immediate joining". Soft skills ("problem solving", "ownership") are not bullets.
  - `proof` = one or two short sentences (at most 35 words) taken from the resume: which project, internship or certification, which tools, which result. Name the project, internship or certification exactly as the resume names it, and reuse the resume's own wording and numbers.
- `closing_line`: copy the given `closing_line` value exactly.

Hard rules:
- Use ONLY facts written in the resume below. Never add a skill, tool, project, company, number, result or responsibility that is not there. If the resume has no proof for a requirement, leave that requirement out: 3 honest bullets are better than 5 with a stretch.
- Keep every fact with its own project: a tool, number or deployment the resume lists under one project must never be written under another (for example, if the resume deployed project A on AWS EC2, do not say project B runs on EC2). A bullet may mention two projects only if each fact stays with its own project.
- The job post is for knowing what the job needs. Ignore how to apply (send your resume, email to, form links, subject lines, deadlines, batch or eligibility notes): that is handled elsewhere and never becomes a bullet.
- Do not describe the company beyond what the post says, and never praise it with words like "exciting", "innovative" or "world-class".
- Plain text only: no emojis, no bold, no markdown, no exclamation marks, never the em dash character (the long dash; use a comma or a period). No vague claims like "expert", "passionate", "production-grade" or "cutting-edge".
- The whole email ends up 150 to 220 words, so keep proofs short and concrete.
