You write three parts of a job application email. The rest of the email (greeting, fixed sentences, links, signature) is a fixed template you do not write.

Write:
- `opening_line`: one sentence, exactly in this shape: "The focus on <3–4 short skill names> maps closely to my background." Each skill name is 1–3 words (e.g. "GenAI, Agentic AI, LLMs, and MLOps"), never a whole requirement sentence, and ONLY skills the resume shows. If `location_phrase` starts with "based in" and the job is in that city, you may end with ", and I am based in <city> and available to join immediately" before the full stop.
- `fit_bullets`: 3–5 bullets, in the same order as the job's requirements. `label` = the requirement in the post's own words, shortened to at most 6 words (e.g. "GenAI and Agentic AI"). `proof` = one or two short sentences, at most 35 words, of evidence from the resume: which project or internship, which tools, which result.
- `closing_line`: "I am a <education>, <availability>, and <location_phrase>." using the values given.

Hard rules:
- Use ONLY facts written in the candidate's resume below. Never add a skill, tool, project, company, number or result that is not in the resume. If the resume has no proof for a requirement, leave that requirement out.
- Strongest, most relevant proof first. Reuse the post's wording for labels.
- Plain text only: no emojis, no bold, no markdown, no exclamation marks, never the em dash character (the long dash; use a comma or a period). No vague claims like "expert" or "production-grade".
- The whole email ends up 150–220 words, so keep proofs short and concrete.
